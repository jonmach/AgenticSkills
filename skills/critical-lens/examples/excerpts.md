# Example excerpts

Four moments that vary most between runs. Each is an output fragment, not a description of a principle. Synthetic content throughout.

---

## 1. A claim that holds up

Synthetic input: an independently funded, pre-registered trial with a published method, 1,200 participants, and a confidence interval that excludes zero.

**Verdict:** Credible (confidence: high — primary paper opened, method public, interval narrow and independent of the vendor)

**Why:**
- Pre-registered, independently funded, and the effect survives the stated subgroup analysis; nothing in the write-up depends on the vendor's own figures.
- The one limitation (single task type, 1,200 participants) bounds transfer rather than credibility, and the authors say so themselves.

**Basis:** Primary paper read, funding statement and methods checked — 8 Nov 2026.

*Prevents:* manufacturing objections to look rigorous. There is nothing to object to, and the verdict says so.

## 2. Steelman first

**On the strongest reading of the claim:** the vendor is comparing against the customer's own pre-deployment baseline, which is the comparison an operations director would actually make, and the pilot ran long enough to cover a full monthly cycle.

**Where it still fails:** the pick-path redesign shipped in the same window, so even the charitable reading cannot separate the software from the process change. The claim is not wrong; it is unattributed.

*Prevents:* attacking a weaker version of the claim, which is easy to win and useless to the reader.

## 3. Genuine conflict between sources

Two studies disagree. Study A (2025, n=84, one firm, vendor-funded) finds a 38% reduction in handling time. Study B (2026, n=1,100 across 14 firms, independent, public method) finds 6% with an interval spanning zero.

This is not a disagreement to average. The studies differ in sample size, number of sites, funding and whether the task was greenfield. Study B is the better evidence for a current decision; Study A is evidence that a large effect is possible under favourable conditions. Reported as a conflict with its cause, not resolved by picking a winner the sources do not support.

*Prevents:* averaging conflicting studies into a meaningless middle number.

## 4. Confidence downgrade after a block

**Verdict:** Plausible but unproven (confidence: low — the funding statement could not be reached, and the one secondary summary contradicts the vendor on sample size)

**Basis:** Vendor page and one aggregator summary read; the linked paper returned 403 twice, so the sample size and funder are unverified. Downgraded from medium on that basis.

*Prevents:* a label that outruns what was actually retrieved, and the temptation to describe a blocked source as "consistent with".
